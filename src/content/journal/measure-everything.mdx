There is a tedious argument in bread that splits people into those who measure
and those who feel. It is a false split. The reason to measure is not that
numbers are better than instinct — it is that a number you have controlled is one
fewer variable competing for your attention.

## What numbers are for

If the flour, the water, the salt and the schedule are fixed, then when a dough
behaves oddly there is a short list of things it can be. Usually the weather. If
none of those are fixed, an odd dough tells you nothing at all, and you spend
months rediscovering the same lesson.

That is the entire argument for the scale. Not precision as an aesthetic —
precision as a way of clearing the field.

## What hands are for

Bulk fermentation is finished when the dough stops fighting back. There is no
number for that. It correlates with time and temperature and volume, and none of
those correlations survive a humid afternoon in Singapore.

So every specification here has two halves, and they carry equal weight:

> 68% hydration · until the dough stops fighting you

The number makes the bake repeatable. The judgement makes it good. Anyone selling
you one without the other is leaving out the difficult half.
