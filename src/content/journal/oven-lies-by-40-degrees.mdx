The dial said 450 °C. The stone said 412 °C. That gap is the difference between a
90-second bake and a 2-minute one, which is the difference between a Neapolitan
and a slightly sad flatbread.

## Reading the floor, not the air

Most domestic ovens report air temperature somewhere near the element. What
matters for pizza is the surface the dough lands on, and a biscotto stone takes a
long time to catch up with the air around it — twenty minutes past the point the
oven says it is ready.

I took six readings with an infrared thermometer at five-minute intervals from
cold, then again after a bake, because the floor drops sharply the moment a cold
ball hits it and the recovery time is the real constraint on how fast you can go.

<Trial label="What this changed">
  I now preheat 20 minutes longer than the oven claims to need, and I leave 90
  seconds between bakes for the floor to recover. Both numbers came from the
  probe, not from a recipe.
</Trial>

The wider point is that every figure in a recipe is measured on someone else's
equipment. Hydration transfers. Times transfer. Temperatures do not, and they are
the ones most likely to be quoted with the most confidence.
