## What the extra day did

Fermentation slows almost to a stop at 4 °C while the enzymes keep working. That
is where flavour is supposed to be built — not by the yeast, which is nearly
dormant, but by the slow breakdown of starch and protein into things that taste
of something.

The structure did exactly what the literature says it should. Cell size up
noticeably against trial 13, walls visibly thinner, and the rim took the heat
without collapsing. What I did not expect was the flavour going *flatter* rather
than deeper — less of the sweetness I get at 24 hours, and a faint sourness that
was not there before.

## Where it went wrong

<Trial label="Discarded — trial 14b">
  Same formula, 72 h cold. The gluten had gone past useful: the ball tore on
  opening and the rim never recovered its structure in the bake.

  Recorded here rather than quietly deleted, because the boundary is the finding.

Next: hold 48 h and drop yeast to 0.09% to see whether the flavour loss tracks
fermentation extent or simply elapsed time. If it is elapsed time, the answer is
that 24 hours was right all along and I have been chasing crumb structure at the
cost of taste.
